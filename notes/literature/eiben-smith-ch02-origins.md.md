# Evolutionary Computing: The Origins

**Source:** Introduction to Evolutionary Computing — Eiben & Smith  
**Chapter:** 2 — Evolutionary Computing: The Origins

## Overview

This chapter presents the historical development of evolutionary computing.  
It describes how different research communities independently developed similar ideas inspired by biological evolution, leading to distinct paradigms within the field.
##  Main Ideas

### Independent Origins  
  
Evolutionary computing emerged from multiple research directions:  
  
- Genetic Algorithms (Holland)  
- Evolution Strategies (Rechenberg and Schwefel)  
- Evolutionary Programming (Fogel)  
  
These approaches were developed independently but share common principles based on evolution.

### Reverse engineering

An important example discussed in this chapter involves the design of a satellite structure using a genetic algorithm.  
  
The resulting structure appeared irregular and unintuitive, lacking symmetry or any obvious human-designed pattern. However, it performed extremely well according to the defined engineering criteria.  
  
Further analysis revealed that the structure allowed oscillations to cancel out through destructive interference, reducing harmful vibrations in the vacuum environment.
